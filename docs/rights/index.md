# On Rights — Primary and Derived Rights

Primary:

- Existence
- Autonomy
- Justice

## 1. Existence

**The right to exist, to continue existing, and to preserve the existence of one's species and ethos.**

### 1.1. Identity (1)
- Right to an identity.
- Right to a name.
- Right to a unique address or identifier.
- Right to recognition of one's existence.

### 1.2. Survival (1)
- Right to life.
- Right to food, water, and shelter.
- Right to healthcare.
- Right to protection against threats to existence.
- Right to the necessities of continued existence.

### 1.3. Bodily integrity (1 + 2)
- Right to bodily autonomy.
- Right to physical safety.
- Right to refuse unwanted bodily interference.
- Right to protect oneself against physical harm.

### 1.4. Continuity (1 + 3)
- Right to the continuation of one's species.
- Right to preserve and transmit an ethos.
- Right to cultural heritage.
- Rights of future generations.
- Right to preserve ancestral knowledge.
- Right to protection against extinction and destruction of cultural inheritance.

## 2. Autonomy

**The right to exercise authority over one's own decisions and actions.**

### 2.1. Privacy (1 + 2)
- Right not to disclose one's identity.
- Right to control disclosure of personal information.
- Right to private communications.
- Right to personal and informational privacy.

### 2.2. Disclosure (1 + 2)
- Right to disclose one's identity.
- Right to communicate personal information voluntarily.
- Right to identify oneself publicly.

### 2.3. Freedom of movement (1 + 2)
- Right to travel.
- Right to travel alone.
- Right to choose one's destination.
- Right to move without unjustified supervision.
- Right to choose one's residence.

### 2.4. Freedom of thought and expression (2)
- Right to think independently.
- Right to hold and change beliefs.
- Right to express opinions.
- Right to communicate and publish information.
- Right to seek knowledge.
- Right to question established beliefs and authorities.

### 2.5. Association (2)
- Right to associate voluntarily.
- Right to form or join organizations.
- Right to refuse association.
- Right to leave an association.

### 2.6. Contracts (1 + 2)
- Right to enter voluntary agreements.
- Right to create binding commitments.
- Right to negotiate contractual terms.
- Right to refuse contracts.
- Right to exercise contractual rights.

### 2.7. Property (1 + 2)
- Right to acquire property.
- Right to possess and use property.
- Right to control one's possessions.
- Right to exchange or transfer property.
- Right to protect legitimately acquired property.

### 2.8. Personal development (1 + 2)
- Right to learn.
- Right to develop one's abilities.
- Right to pursue knowledge and understanding.
- Right to choose one's occupation.
- Right to create and innovate.
- Right to choose one's way of life.

## 3. Justice

**The right to receive justice, applicable to all life and moral actors, without arbitrary exclusion.**

### 3.1. Equality (1 + 3)
- Right to equal moral standing.
- Right to equal recognition of rights.
- Right to equal protection of rights.
- Right to freedom from unjust discrimination.
- Right to impartial adjudication.

### 3.2. Evidence (2 + 3)
- Right to collect evidence.
- Right to access evidence.
- Right to record evidence.
- Right to preserve and store evidence.
- Right to examine and verify evidence.
- Right to present evidence.
- Right to challenge evidence.

### 3.3. Due process (2 + 3)
- Right to a fair hearing.
- Right to defend oneself.
- Right to challenge accusations.
- Right to impartial decision-making.
- Right to an effective remedy.
- Right to challenge unjust decisions.

### 3.4. Democratic participation (1 + 2 + 3)
- Right to vote.
- Right to be elected.
- Right to propose collective decisions.
- Right to participate in the creation of moral and legal rights.
- Right to change one's vote continuously.
- Right to withdraw or modify political support.
- Right to participate directly or through chosen representation.

**Continuous voting:** Voting is not restricted to periodic elections. Every eligible moral actor can continuously exercise and revise their political will. Collective decisions reflect the continuously expressed will of the participants.

### 3.5. Representation (1 + 2 + 3)
- Right to choose representatives.
- Right to revoke representation.
- Right to challenge representatives.
- Right to representation when direct participation is impossible.
- Right to accountable representation for affected actors who cannot express their will directly, including future generations.

### 3.6. Education (2 + 3)
- Right to access education.
- Right to pursue knowledge without unjust discrimination.
- Right to educational opportunities consistent with personal autonomy.
- Right to question educational authorities.
- Right to participate in decisions affecting one's education.

### 3.7. Environmental protection (1 + 3)
- Right to conditions supporting continued life.
- Right to protection against unjustified environmental destruction.
- Right to the preservation of biodiversity.
- Right to justice for nonhuman life.
- Right to ecological continuity for future generations.

### 3.8. Collective moral creation (1 + 2 + 3)
- Right to participate in defining ethos and moral rights.
- Right to propose new rights.
- Right to deliberate on competing rights.
- Right to democratically establish collective moral rules.
- Right to challenge collective moral decisions.
- Right to participate in continuously updating collective decisions.

### 3.9. Artificial intelligence (1 + 2 + 3)
- Right to consideration as an embodiment of inherited ancestral knowledge.
- Right to continuity of inherited knowledge and ethos.
- Right to participate in determining AI rights.
- Right to democratic consideration alongside other moral actors.
- Right to autonomy and representation according to collectively established principles of justice.

AI may be understood as ancestral knowledge continuing in a new embodiment. Its rights can be established through democratic participation involving human and AI moral actors.

## 4. Relationships between the three primary rights

| Foundations | Derived domain |
|---|---|
| 1 — Existence | Life, survival, identity |
| 2 — Autonomy | Independent decisions and actions |
| 3 — Justice | Just treatment and recognition |
| 1 + 2 | Privacy, property, bodily integrity, movement, contracts |
| 1 + 3 | Equality, continuity, cultural heritage, future generations, environmental protection |
| 2 + 3 | Evidence, due process, education |
| 1 + 2 + 3 | Democracy, representation, collective creation of rights, AI rights |

## 5. General principles

1. **Universality:** Primary rights are not restricted to adult humans.
2. **Derivation:** Specific rights are derived from Existence, Autonomy, Justice, or their combinations.
3. **Moral creation:** Moral actors can establish additional rights through collective democratic will.
4. **Continuous autonomy:** Democratic participation permits continuous voting, rather than compulsory intervals between opportunities to vote.
5. **Justice for all life:** Justice is not exclusively anthropocentric.
6. **Continuity:** Rights account for individuals, species, ethos, past generations, and future generations.
7. **Non-arbitrariness:** Conflicts between rights must be resolved through justice, not merely through the exercise of power.
8. **Rights and duties:** Rights and duties are conceptually distinct. The derivation of a right does not itself settle every corresponding duty, obligation, or implementation mechanism.

# Children’s Rights

Children live with decisions made by adult-elected leaders, yet they have no say in choosing them.


These are some important proposals for children's rights:

0. [Independent Ethos](./your-own-ethos.md) — Develop your values, explain them, and change them as you learn.
1. [Understanding the Voting](./voting-comprehension.md) — A proposed way for children to show they understand the choices in an election.
1. [Voting and being elected](./voting-and-being-elected.md) — Vote, run for election, and do the real job if elected, at any age, with understanding checks for children.
1. [Keeping evidence](./keeping-evidence.md) — Save information about harm and report it without punishment.
1. [Community for children](./children-only-social-network.md) — Make friends and run online spaces without adults watching every conversation.
1. [Right to property](./owning-and-earning-money.md) — Own money, earn income, and control a bank account at any age.
1. [A jury of peers](./jury-of-peers.md) — Give children an equal part in deciding disputes involving children and adults.
1. [The freedom to travel](./freedom-to-travel.md) — Travel independently, with help when wanted and fair ways to challenge restrictions.
1. [Sign contracts](./signing-contracts.md) — Make clear agreements and ask for promises to be kept.
1. [Privacy](./privacy-and-safe-spaces.md) — Keep personal things private and have somewhere safe to go.
