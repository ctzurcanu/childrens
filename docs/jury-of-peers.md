# A fair jury that includes children

**The idea: children should have the right to a jury of peers. In a dispute between children and adults, the jury should include equal numbers of both, with equal voting power.**

A **jury** is a group that listens to evidence and helps decide a case. Its members are **jurors**. **Peers** are people whose position or experience makes them suitable equals for this role. Exactly how to choose peers is still an open question.

This is a proposal for change, not a description of how juries work today.

## What would make the jury fair?

- **Independent selection:** the parents, teachers, or organizations involved in the case could not choose the jurors.
- **Equal participation:** child jurors would receive the same evidence, ask questions about it, and take a full part in the discussion before the decision.
- **Clear explanations:** help would make the proceedings understandable without letting adults make child jurors’ decisions for them.
- **Freedom to decide:** jurors must not have ties to the people involved that could make their judgment unfair. They must be protected from threats and punishment.
- **Shared decision-making:** neither the children nor the adults could impose a decision alone.

If the jury could not reach a decision under the agreed rules, there would be a set process for review or a new hearing. This situation is called a **deadlock**.

## An example

A child has a dispute with a school. Under this proposal, the jury would include equal numbers of children and adults, each with equal voting power.

The school could not avoid this rule by saying, “We are an organization, not an adult.” Disputes with schools, police forces, and other adult-run institutions would count as child–adult disputes.

## Questions still to answer

- Who counts as a peer, and how should jurors be selected?
- Which kinds of cases would use these juries?
- How many votes would be needed for a decision?
- How would deadlocks, reviews, and new hearings work?
