# The freedom to travel

**The idea: children should be free to move around and travel, including on their own.**

Under this proposal, stopping a journey would require evidence of a specific danger. A restriction must fit that danger, be explained, and be open to independent review. Age alone would not be enough.

This is a proposal for change, not a description of today’s travel laws.

## What would this include?

- Children could obtain travel documents, buy tickets, use transport, and arrange a place to stay without a blanket rule requiring parental permission.
- Route planning, clear information, and emergency support would be available. Help would not automatically require an adult companion or parental tracking.
- Children could challenge being stopped from leaving a home, school, or institution, especially when trying to reach safety or report harm.
- Decisions would consider the particular person and journey. One child’s need for help would not justify stopping every child.
- A child who travels independently would keep the right to return to their home, receive care, and access education.

## An example

A child can manage a particular train journey. Under this proposal, “You are a child” would not be enough reason to refuse them a ticket. Anyone stopping the journey would need to explain the specific risk and why the restriction fits it.

The child could ask an independent reviewer to reconsider. The review would need to happen soon enough to make a difference before the journey becomes impossible.

## Independent help with travel rights

A proposed service would help children obtain documents and urgently challenge refusals by guardians or travel companies. It would be independent of whoever imposed the restriction.

## Questions still to answer

- How should risks for a particular journey be assessed?
- How could urgent reviews happen quickly enough?
- How would children access accommodation and emergency support?
- How would this work with border rules, travel documents, and rules about who is legally responsible for a child’s care?
