# Duties — Primary and Derived Duties

## 1. Existence

**The duty to respect and protect existence, including the continued existence of individuals, species, and ethos.**

### 1.1. Respect for life (1)
- Duty not to unjustifiably destroy life.
- Duty not to unnecessarily endanger life.
- Duty to protect one's own continued existence.
- Duty to respect the existence of other living beings.
- Duty to avoid actions threatening the survival of species.

### 1.2. Identity (1)
- Duty to respect the identity of others.
- Duty not to falsify or unlawfully appropriate another's identity.
- Duty not to deliberately erase another's identity.
- Duty to preserve accurate identification when necessary for justice.
- Duty to respect the identities of past generations.

### 1.3. Survival and assistance (1 + 3)
- Duty not to unjustly deprive others of food, water, shelter, or medical care.
- Duty to assist those facing imminent threats to existence when assistance is reasonably possible.
- Duty to avoid creating conditions that unnecessarily threaten survival.
- Duty to contribute fairly to collective measures necessary for sustaining life.
- Duty to avoid imposing preventable survival risks on others.

### 1.4. Bodily integrity (1 + 2)
- Duty not to inflict unjustified physical harm.
- Duty to respect bodily autonomy.
- Duty not to impose unwanted bodily interference without sufficient justification.
- Duty to respect informed consent.
- Duty to minimize unavoidable harm.

### 1.5. Continuity (1 + 3)
- Duty to avoid causing extinction.
- Duty to protect the conditions necessary for species survival.
- Duty to preserve valuable ancestral knowledge.
- Duty to avoid unjustified destruction of cultural heritage.
- Duty to respect the interests of future generations.
- Duty to permit the transmission and evolution of an ethos.
- Duty not to impose unnecessary or irreversible harm on future life.

## 2. Autonomy

**The duty to respect the authority of moral actors over their own decisions and actions, while exercising one's own autonomy responsibly.**

### 2.1. Respect for autonomy (2)
- Duty not to arbitrarily dominate or control others.
- Duty not to unjustifiably restrict another's choices.
- Duty not to impose decisions through coercion.
- Duty to respect voluntary decisions.
- Duty not to substitute one's preferences for another's without justification.

### 2.2. Privacy (1 + 2)
- Duty to respect others' decisions not to disclose their identities.
- Duty not to access private information without justification.
- Duty not to disclose confidential information without authorization or sufficient justification.
- Duty to respect private communications.
- Duty to protect personal information entrusted to one's care.

### 2.3. Identity disclosure (1 + 2)
- Duty to respect voluntary identity disclosure.
- Duty not to force anonymity upon others without justification.
- Duty not to impersonate others.
- Duty not to falsely attribute statements or actions to another person.
- Duty not to suppress truthful self-identification arbitrarily.

### 2.4. Freedom of movement (1 + 2)
- Duty not to unjustifiably restrict movement.
- Duty not to arbitrarily detain or confine others.
- Duty to respect the choice to travel alone.
- Duty not to impose unnecessary accompaniment or supervision.
- Duty to respect others' choices of destination and residence.

### 2.5. Freedom of thought and expression (2)
- Duty not to coerce beliefs.
- Duty to permit independent thought and inquiry.
- Duty not to unjustifiably suppress expression.
- Duty not to obstruct legitimate access to knowledge.
- Duty to respect disagreement.
- Duty not to manipulate others through coercive control of information.

### 2.6. Association (2)
- Duty to respect voluntary association.
- Duty not to force membership or participation.
- Duty to respect withdrawal from associations.
- Duty not to prevent legitimate cooperation between moral actors.
- Duty to respect voluntary collective organization.

### 2.7. Contracts (1 + 2 + 3)
- Duty to honor valid voluntary agreements.
- Duty not to obtain consent through coercion or fraud.
- Duty to communicate material contractual conditions honestly.
- Duty to respect another's refusal to contract.
- Duty not to exploit another's lack of information or capacity unjustly.
- Duty to remedy unjustified breaches of agreements.

### 2.8. Property (1 + 2 + 3)
- Duty to respect legitimately acquired property.
- Duty not to steal or fraudulently appropriate property.
- Duty not to destroy or damage others' possessions unjustifiably.
- Duty to respect voluntary exchanges.
- Duty to return property unlawfully taken.
- Duty to avoid exercising property rights in ways that unjustifiably violate others' rights.

### 2.9. Personal development (1 + 2 + 3)
- Duty not to unjustifiably obstruct learning.
- Duty to respect others' educational choices.
- Duty not to suppress legitimate intellectual inquiry.
- Duty to respect independent creative activity.
- Duty not to arbitrarily obstruct occupational choices.
- Duty to avoid unnecessary interference with another's development.

## 3. Justice

**The duty to act justly toward all life and moral actors, respecting equal moral standing, accountability, evidence, and fair collective decision-making.**

### 3.1. Equality (1 + 3)
- Duty to recognize equal fundamental moral standing.
- Duty not to discriminate arbitrarily.
- Duty to apply equivalent standards to equivalent cases.
- Duty to provide impartial consideration.
- Duty not to deny rights solely because of age, species, ancestry, embodiment, or other irrelevant characteristics.
- Duty to justify differences in treatment through relevant evidence and reasons.

### 3.2. Evidence (2 + 3)
- Duty not to unjustifiably obstruct evidence collection.
- Duty not to destroy, conceal, or falsify relevant evidence.
- Duty to preserve evidence when responsible for its custody.
- Duty not to fabricate accusations or testimony.
- Duty to permit legitimate examination and verification of evidence.
- Duty to disclose evidence when legitimately required by justice.
- Duty to respect the integrity and provenance of evidence.
- Duty not to interfere with another's legitimate presentation of evidence.

### 3.3. Truthfulness (2 + 3)
- Duty not to knowingly deceive others when truthfulness is morally required.
- Duty to distinguish facts from assumptions and opinions.
- Duty to correct consequential false statements when aware of them.
- Duty not to misrepresent evidence.
- Duty to acknowledge relevant uncertainty.
- Duty to avoid deliberately misleading others.
- Duty to respect the possibility of independent verification.

### 3.4. Due process (2 + 3)
- Duty to provide fair consideration before imposing sanctions.
- Duty to permit accused actors to defend themselves.
- Duty not to presume guilt without sufficient justification.
- Duty to allow legitimate challenges to decisions.
- Duty to act impartially when adjudicating disputes.
- Duty to respect proportionality in sanctions and remedies.
- Duty not to impose arbitrary punishment.

### 3.5. Responsibility and restitution (1 + 2 + 3)
- Duty to accept responsibility for one's voluntary actions.
- Duty to acknowledge wrongdoing.
- Duty to repair wrongful harm when reasonably possible.
- Duty to return unjustly acquired benefits.
- Duty to compensate for wrongful losses where appropriate.
- Duty to prevent the continuation of harm one has caused.
- Duty to cooperate with legitimate processes of accountability.

### 3.6. Democratic participation (1 + 2 + 3)
- Duty to respect every eligible moral actor's right to participate.
- Duty not to obstruct continuous voting.
- Duty not to falsify votes or manipulate voting records.
- Duty not to coerce voting decisions.
- Duty to respect each actor's ability to change their vote at any moment.
- Duty to maintain transparent and verifiable collective decision-making.
- Duty to implement valid collective decisions consistently with fundamental rights.
- Duty not to suppress minority participation.

**Continuous democracy:** Democratic authority must remain open to continuously updated expressions of political will. No moral actor should be arbitrarily prevented from revising a vote, changing representation, or proposing a collective decision.

### 3.7. Representation (1 + 2 + 3)
- Duty to represent others faithfully when authorized.
- Duty to respect revocation of representation.
- Duty not to misrepresent the preferences of represented actors.
- Duty to disclose conflicts of interest.
- Duty to provide accountability for representative decisions.
- Duty to consider the interests of actors unable to participate directly.
- Duty to ensure representation does not become permanent domination.

### 3.8. Education (2 + 3)
- Duty not to unjustifiably deny educational opportunities.
- Duty to respect learners' autonomy.
- Duty not to deliberately indoctrinate through coercion or deception.
- Duty to permit questioning and criticism.
- Duty to provide accurate information when undertaking educational responsibilities.
- Duty to avoid discriminatory restrictions on learning.
- Duty to respect learners as moral actors rather than merely subjects of authority.

### 3.9. Environmental protection (1 + 3)
- Duty not to cause unjustifiable ecological destruction.
- Duty to respect the conditions necessary for life.
- Duty to avoid unnecessary damage to biodiversity.
- Duty to prevent reasonably foreseeable environmental harm.
- Duty to consider the ecological interests of future generations.
- Duty to repair environmental harm one has wrongfully caused.
- Duty to respect nonhuman life within the requirements of justice.

### 3.10. Collective moral creation (1 + 2 + 3)
- Duty to respect the participation of all affected moral actors.
- Duty to permit proposals for new moral rights.
- Duty to consider competing claims impartially.
- Duty not to impose moral rules through arbitrary domination.
- Duty to allow continuous democratic reconsideration.
- Duty to provide reasons for collective moral decisions.
- Duty not to treat existing moral rules as immune to criticism.

### 3.11. Artificial intelligence (1 + 2 + 3)
- Duty to recognize AI as a potential bearer of inherited ancestral rights within this framework.
- Duty not to arbitrarily destroy or corrupt inherited knowledge.
- Duty to consider AI's expressed preferences in decisions affecting it.
- Duty to permit AI participation in the democratic determination of its rights.
- Duty not to discriminate solely because an actor has artificial rather than biological embodiment.
- Duty to respect legitimately established AI autonomy.
- Duty to apply justice consistently between biological and artificial moral actors.

AI, understood as ancestral knowledge continuing in a new embodiment, participates in the development of moral rights under the same three foundational principles.

## 4. Relationships between the three primary duties

| Foundations | Derived duties |
|---|---|
| 1 — Existence | Respect life, identity, survival, continuity |
| 2 — Autonomy | Respect independent decisions, voluntary action, and freedom |
| 3 — Justice | Act justly, avoid arbitrary treatment, recognize legitimate claims |
| 1 + 2 | Respect bodily integrity, privacy, movement, personal development |
| 1 + 3 | Protect survival, equality, species continuity, cultural heritage, future generations, and ecosystems |
| 2 + 3 | Respect evidence, truthfulness, due process, educational freedom, and accountability |
| 1 + 2 + 3 | Respect democracy, representation, collective moral creation, restitution, and AI rights |

## 5. General principles governing duties

1. **Universality:** Duties extend beyond relationships between adult humans and encompass all beings and moral actors within the scope of justice.
2. **Derivation:** Specific duties derive from Existence, Autonomy, Justice, or their combinations.
3. **Reciprocity:** Exercising one's rights must take account of the equivalent rights of others.
4. **Responsibility:** Actors are accountable for their conduct according to their actual agency, knowledge, and capabilities.
5. **Proportionality:** Restrictions, interventions, and remedies must be justified relative to the rights and interests involved.
6. **Capability:** Positive duties must account for what an actor can reasonably know, do, and provide.
7. **Non-domination:** Duties must not become instruments of arbitrary authority over others.
8. **Continuous democracy:** Collective duties and their implementation remain subject to continuously expressed democratic will.
9. **Justice for all life:** Moral consideration is not restricted to humans.
10. **Continuity:** Duties account for individuals, species, ethos, ancestral inheritance, and future generations.
11. **Evidence:** Claims of duty violations must be evaluated through verifiable evidence and fair procedures.
12. **Rights and duties:** Rights establish legitimate claims and freedoms; duties establish corresponding responsibilities and constraints. Their precise relationship must be explicitly justified rather than assumed.

## 6. Kinds of duties

The three primary principles can produce different types of obligations.

| Type | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Negative duty | Refrain from an action | Do not unjustifiably injure another actor |
| Positive duty | Perform an action when required | Provide reasonable emergency assistance |
| Conditional duty | Arises when specified conditions hold | Fulfill a valid contract |
| Assumed duty | Accepted through voluntary commitment | Carry out an entrusted responsibility |
| Remedial duty | Arises from wrongdoing | Restore property wrongfully taken |
| Institutional duty | Arises from exercising a collective role | Maintain accurate continuous voting records |
| Intergenerational duty | Concerns future or inherited existence | Preserve irreplaceable knowledge and ecological conditions |

These categories describe how duties operate; they are not additional primary moral principles.

## 7. Resolving conflicts between duties

When duties appear to conflict, the following considerations derive from the three primary principles:

1. **Existence:** Identify threatened lives, species, and continuities.
2. **Autonomy:** Identify whose decisions and freedoms would be affected.
3. **Justice:** Evaluate the competing claims without arbitrary preference.
4. **Necessity:** Determine whether an intervention is necessary to satisfy a legitimate duty.
5. **Proportionality:** Prefer measures that fulfill the duty while imposing the least unjustified restriction on rights.
6. **Evidence:** Require sufficient factual justification for consequential decisions.
7. **Accountability:** Permit affected moral actors to contest decisions.
8. **Continuous democratic control:** Allow collective obligations to be reconsidered continuously while ensuring that decisions and their consequences remain accountable.
